04The number that actually decides it
Section 301 vessel fee

The fee is at zero today and reverts on 9 November unless it is renewed. On a VLCC that is the difference between a normal US call and no US call at all.

Market context — desk-entered marks

Approval and the Hormuz premium are not the same story, and it would be dishonest to draw them on one axis — approval is a slow political variable, Hormuz a fast physical one. What links them is the policy reaction function: an administration at −29 on tariffs and −28 on inflation has a strong incentive to be seen bringing shipping and energy costs down, and a weak one to add a fresh cost to every China-built ship calling the US in the same quarter.

05The November window
Six days, two decisions

Two dates, six days apart, and the second one is ours.

TUE 3 NOV 2026 · 62 DAYS

Midterm elections

Approval at 33% sits below the level that cost the president’s party 41 House seats in 2018, and independents have moved with it. A heavy loss argues for a pivot to cost-of-living wins; a narrow one argues for escalation. Neither is neutral for the China-nexus fleet.

MON 9 NOV 2026 · 68 DAYS

Section 301 truce expires

The one-year mutual suspension agreed on 10 November 2025 runs out. Absent a renewal, the USTR fee schedule resumes against Chinese-built and Chinese-operated tonnage — and China’s mirror fee resumes against US-built, US-owned and US-operated ships.

Desk implication. Any period charter or S&P negotiation on China-built tonnage that straddles 9 November carries an unpriced binary. On a 300,000 dwt VLCC of roughly 156,000 net tons, a resumed $80/NT call fee is about $12.5m per US port call — not a rate adjustment, a trade exclusion. Charterers will want the clause; owners of Korean and Japanese tonnage should be pricing the scarcity now.

Method note — read before circulating. This is a demo build, and three things need saying plainly.

1. The chart shows only published readings. Seven of them, plotted where they fall. Earlier drafts drew a smooth monthly line between them; that line was interpolated and has been removed. Where more than a hundred days separate two readings the connector is dashed, because we have nothing to show in between.

2. The two rate marks were entered by hand from secondary reporting, not read off a terminal, and the TD3C figure in particular is an extraordinary number that deserves checking against Baltic before anyone quotes it. They are tagged Desk-entered for that reason. Wire this page to a live Baltic and Drewry feed before it goes anywhere near a client.

3. The transmission board and the November window are opinion — ours. The poll numbers in them are published; the channels, the exposures and the recommended actions are a desk view and should be argued with.
