02  Every issue is underwater. Four of them are ours.
Net approval by issue, 2026
Transmits directly to freight Background political weather

Tariffs are the president’s worst-polling policy at −29, worse than inflation. That matters to a broker for one reason: a policy this unpopular is either abandoned or doubled down on, and both paths move tonne-miles. Immigration at −13 remains the strongest card in the deck, which is why it, and not trade, is the likely centre of the autumn campaign.

04  What the market has already paid for it
Latest published assessments

The approval line and the TD3C line are not the same story, and it would be dishonest to draw them on one axis. Approval is a slow political variable; Hormuz is a fast physical one. What links them is the policy reaction function — an administration at −29 on tariffs and −28 on inflation has a strong incentive to be seen bringing energy and shipping costs down, and a weak incentive to add a new cost to every China-built box ship calling the US in the same quarter.

05  The November window
Six days, two decisions
TUE 3 NOV 2026 · 63 DAYS

Midterm elections

Approval at 33% is below the level that cost the president’s party 41 House seats in 2018, and independents have moved with it. A heavy loss argues for a pivot to cost-of-living wins; a narrow one argues for escalation. Neither is neutral for the China-nexus fleet.

MON 9 NOV 2026 · 69 DAYS

Section 301 truce expires

The one-year mutual suspension agreed on 10 November 2025 runs out. Absent a renewal, the USTR fee schedule resumes against Chinese-built and Chinese-operated tonnage — and China’s mirror fee resumes against US-built, US-owned and US-operated ships.

Desk implication. Any period charter or S&P negotiation on China-built tonnage that straddles 9 November carries an unpriced binary. On a 300,000 dwt VLCC of roughly 156,000 net tons, a resumed $80/NT call fee is on the order of $12.5m per US port call — which is not a rate adjustment, it is a trade-exclusion. Charterers will want the clause; owners of Korean and Japanese tonnage should be pricing the scarcity now.
